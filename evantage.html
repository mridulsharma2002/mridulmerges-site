EVantage

UNB capstone · 2025

A booking and pricing system for campus EV chargers. The price follows the grid: it goes up when emissions and demand peak, and down when the grid is clean and quiet.

The shape is illustrative, not data from the project.

Why I built it

Charging a Tesla Model Y once (66.25 kWh) could power an average New Brunswick home for over 16 hours. Put a few of those on one campus lot at five o'clock and you have a real load on the grid, at exactly the time it's working hardest and burning the most fuel.

The chargers on our campus had a second problem: cars parked in charging spots that weren't supposed to be there. So my team and I asked whether the price itself could do the work. Make charging cheaper when the grid is clean and quiet, more expensive at the peak, and let people book a time instead of hunting for a free spot.

It's the same idea Tesla now uses at its Superchargers, where the price changes with the time of day and how busy a site is. We built it for one parking lot, as students, over eight months.

What I did

I was the team's data analyst and a developer. I worked on the system logic, the data model, and the forecasting that predicts when the peak will hit, so the price can move before the grid gets busy instead of after.
